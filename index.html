Bend, don’t break →
Graceful degradation as a chip-design feature — reliability past P99 with no added area, worked through AI inference.

A generic truth about complex chips on shared resources: you can’t provision the P100 tail, because it lives in contention on shared compute, not the datapath. Build a throttle into the datapath and the same silicon stays reliable past P99 for free — and the headroom you reclaim becomes a tiered-QoS product. Four live explorers: token-deadline anytime degradation, serving-side admission control vs congestion collapse, the capacity-vs-tail-guarantee payoff sold as QoS, and the self-correcting speculative throttle. Plus a comparison of which degradation lever hurts least.

Small Frees Big →
Why data movement, not arithmetic, is the whole bill for AI inference — and the shape that follows.

Rare earth isn't rare — you just pay to move mountains of ore for a trace of metal. Modern inference runs on the same economics: the arithmetic is nearly free, and moving the data is the whole bill. One argument in two moves — small, targeted compute is the hero, and the next move is pushing some of it onto the memory itself — the shape RAN reached first.
Explainer · memory physics · new
The array can’t keep up →
Interface speed is racing; the DRAM core has been flat for twenty years — why bandwidth is bought, not clocked.

The physical foundation under the data-movement thesis: the three “bandwidths” people conflate, why the DRAM array core is stuck near ~50 ns while per-pin rate, width, and modulation climb ~100×, why 224G SerDes and co-packaged optics are a different (scale-out) axis, and the huge internal bandwidth that near-memory compute exists to tap. Leaves exactly two durable moves — move fewer bytes, and compute before the pins.
Compute datapath & numerics · new
Block float, rediscovered →
The frontier inference numeric — MXFP4 microscaling — is block floating point, which comms and DSP silicon have shipped for fifty years.

A translation from multi-precision vector datapaths to the DL tensor engine: why MX / microscaling is block float at 32-element granularity, how an MX MAC is an int8×int4 tile plus a per-block exponent, one FFN down-projection tile mapped end-to-end at MXFP4, and the reframe that low precision is first a bandwidth lever for decode and only second a MAC-area lever for prefill. On the numeric the field is most excited about, the block-float lineage is a head start, not a gap.

Essay · methodology & economics
The digital twin, finally affordable →
Why AI coding agents change the economics of silicon re-spins — and why AI without the methodology is a liability.

A leading-node premium chip costs ~$1B, mostly engineering labor, and labor is heads × time — where time is dominated by re-spins. The cure (a pre-silicon digital twin, so bring-up is confirmation not discovery) always worked but was too expensive to always build. AI collapses that cost, making re-spin avoidance the default. Evidence-led: a near-memory datapath taken solo to a filed patent, verified RTL, open CI, and a bit-exact hardware-in-the-loop cosim.
