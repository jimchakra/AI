02The problem — moving the numbers, not computing them

On a modern process the multiply-accumulate at the heart of inference is nearly free; fetching its operands is the whole bill. A read decomposes honestly into an unavoidable DRAM-array access plus transport to wherever the compute sits — and near-memory removes only the transport. For memory-bound decode, where each weight is fetched and used once, movement runs above 90% of the energy. Turn the knobs:

03The gate — a lossless→lossy knob

Complete the reduction on the memory side, then gate the outputs there and return only survivors. At threshold θ=0 the gate removes exactly what ReLU already zeroes — bit-exact, lossless. 03bThe input-side gate — the bitmap as a read-mask

The move that turns the gate from a 2nd-order return trick into a 1st-order energy lever — honestly. The gated FFN-hidden vector is the input to the down-projection matmul, and its survivor bitmap (the very one the encoder already builds for the compressed return) is exactly the set of down-projection weight columns worth fetching. Spend the sparsity there and you skip weight reads, not result writes.

Same gate, ~1000× the energy. Accounted on the return path, gating 79% of activations saved ~0.01% of decode energy (activations are ~0.01% of movement). Accounted as a down-projection read-mask, the same 79% skips ~12% of total decode energy at 3% quality — ~10% at 2%. The quality curve is identical (zeroing a hidden value is the same operation both ways); only the accounting differs, because now you avoid fetching the operands those zeros would have multiplied. θ=0 is lossless; ReLU-FFN models get a lossless slice for free, GeLU needs θ>0. Honest bounds: this skips the down-projection only (~⅓ of weight reads); the up-projection input is dense and attention weights are untouched, so the ceiling here is ~15%. The larger prize is the same idea on the KV reads (the biggest term, ~38 mJ) — that needs a predictor, not a bitmap. Next build. First-order model on a 7B/int4/HBM/8K point; quality measured on the proxy co-sim.

What the real model says — honestly. Three findings, two of them course-corrections: (1) the toy overstated FFN gate-tolerance. On the proxy, 79% FFN sparsity cost ~3% quality; on the real model that level costs ~20%+, and the usable point is ~50% sparse for +1.7% perplexity → 6.6% of total decode energy. Reporting that downward revision is the point of validating on a real model. (2) KV is the dominant lever. It’s 55% of the budget, and dropping just the oldest quarter of the KV cache costs +4.8% perplexity for 13.5% total energy — roughly double the FFN read-mask — scaling to 30–48% at higher quality cost. (3) Content-aware selection all but erases the cost — measured, not asserted. The sink+window policy above is content-independent (drop old tokens blindly). Swapping in a Quest-style content-aware page selector — summarize each KV page by per-channel min/max keys, score every query against them, read only the top-k pages (plus the sink page and the query's own page) — keeps the important old tokens. At the same ~75% KV kept it costs +0.12% perplexity instead of +4.8% — a 40× quality win for the identical byte budget (table above), and the advantage holds all the way down (17× at 44% kept, 8× at 23%). Which bytes you skip dominates how many. The energy figure already subtracts the per-page min/max summary tax (≈1/P of KV) so it is an honest apples-to-apples vs the blind floor. The two levers attack different terms (down-proj weights vs KV reads), so they stack: ~20% of total decode energy for ~6% quality, on top of the θ-independent 3.5–17.5× locality. Caveat carried for the reviewer: the KV keep-fraction is measured on a 2K window while energy is modeled at 32K deployment — directionally conservative; a native long-context run would refine it. This is the near-memory hardware realization of the contextual-sparsity that large transformers exhibit (Deja Vu, ICML 2023; the lazy-neuron phenomenon, 2022; StreamingLLM; H2O). Reproduce: m4_study.py. The content-aware selector’s hardware cost — block/timing diagrams, ~12.5% summary storage, sub-0.001 mm² selector, and the 1/P read tax — is speced in the KV-selector coprocessor note.

What the cut buys — reads down, throughput up

Two framings of the same measured result, both first-order and regime-dependent. Read reduction: content-aware KV selection reads ~43% of the KV at +0.97% perplexity (a 57% cut), falling to ~30% kept (~70% cut) at ~3%; the FFN survivor-bitmap read-mask skips ~50% of the down-projection columns at +1.7% (~15% of total weight reads — down-projection only). Because the KV and weight levers are asymmetric, the overall memory-read cut depends on the weight-vs-KV mix, which batch size and context length set:

RegimeKV share of readsOverall read cut (≤3% ppl)Queries at fixed BW & power
Batch-1, short context~15%~20–30%~1.25–1.4×
Batch-1, 32K context~50%~35–45%~1.5–1.8×
Batched, long context (serving)~85–95%~55–65%~2.2–2.9×
Why a read cut becomes throughput. In memory-bound decode, per-token time and energy scale with bytes moved (data movement is >90% of that energy — Finding 01), and the compute units sit idle waiting on memory. So cutting read volume by a fraction f lets the same memory bandwidth and power serve ≈ 1/(1−f) as many queries: 20% cut → ~1.25× (+25%), 60% → ~2.5×, 70% → ~3.3× (+230%). The gain is largest in batched long-context serving, because that is where KV — the content-selectable term batching cannot amortize — dominates the traffic; the weight read-mask adds a smaller, bounded piece (~15%, down-projection only). Bounds, stated: first-order (static/leakage power does not scale, so the per-watt gain runs a little below 1/(1−f)); the selection compute and the ≈1/P summary-read tax are already subtracted; the cut becomes a real bandwidth saving (not merely fewer FLOPs) only when the skip is enforced at the memory, per the datapath below; and the keep/quality figures are measured on Qwen2.5-1.5B / WikiText-2 — directional, not a 70B/128K guarantee.

04From thesis to silicon — the datapath

One tile.yaml single source of truth fans out to RTL parameters, an FW header, and DV config (a drift fails CI). The tile is three stages, and the last carries the real clock-domain crossing:

STAGE 1
Compute PE
int8×int4 MAC → cross-bank distributed reduction → requantize → in-situ ReLU/threshold gate.
→
STAGE 2
Encoder
Compressed return: survivor bitmap + packed non-zero values. Only survivors leave the tile.
→
STAGE 3
Packetizer + CDC
Link-layer framer (header/bitmap/survivors) across a Gray-pointer async FIFO — the memory-clk ↔ link-clk crossing.
Compressed return isn't free. Each tile packet carries a fixed 12 B overhead (4 B header + 8 B bitmap). It only pays once gated: ≈1.19× vs a dense return at 0% sparsity (worse), 0.44× at the ~80% sweet spot, 0.19× at 100%. The break-even is stated, not hidden.

07Hardware-in-the-loop — is it right, on something real?

Beyond random-vector regression: a 64×128 FFN sub-tile of a trained transformer, quantized to the tile's int8×int4 format, computed by the actual nmgr_pe RTL simulation and read back into the forward pass. Result — 64/64 outputs bit-exact to the numpy golden on real model data (55% survivor sparsity; dequantized vs FP relu(Wx) cosine 0.99, rel-L2 0.16 from int4). The block is a verified drop-in for the layer.

08How this was decided and built

Two disciplines behind the work, detailed in the methodology: deciding the architecture on simulation evidence with the biases named (static latency is over-optimistic, static peak over-pessimistic; a discrete-event contention model corrects both), and executing it as concurrent RTL / FW / DV from one source of truth — a cycle-accurate SystemC model and an ISS running real firmware form a hardware/firmware digital twin the team develops against pre-silicon, so bring-up is confirmation, not discovery. The single-source fan-out and the golden-gated harness are exactly the "code-like" layer an AI assistant accelerates — the value being the harness (bit-exact golden, lint, exhaustive sim, LEC) that makes generated RTL trustworthy.
